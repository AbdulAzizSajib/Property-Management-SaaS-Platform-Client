## Context

`HeroSection.tsx` currently has a working prototype from an earlier iteration: a single `useScroll({ target: sectionRef, offset: ["start start", "end start"] })` drives a background `scale`/`y`/`opacity` transform and a preview-screenshot `scale` transform, both animating in-place. That prototype does not satisfy `landing/hero-scroll-reveal` — the background must not scale or fade, and the screenshot's scale range/trigger points don't match the required behavior. This design replaces that prototype's scroll wiring.

An earlier revision of this design tried a plain CSS `position: fixed` background with no scroll-linked movement at all. That approach was implemented and visually tested, and failed on two counts: (1) `position: fixed` is positioned relative to the viewport, not the hero section, so it rendered across the entire page instead of staying confined to the hero; and (2) with zero movement, the `object-cover object-top` crop never changed, so only the top of the tall source photo (sky) was ever visible — the flower field lower in the image never appeared. Both are corrected by this revision.

A second visual pass found the screenshot's scale was driven by the whole hero section's scroll range (`sectionRef`), which spans the headline, sub-headline, and CTAs above the screenshot. Because that content sits well above the screenshot in the section's own height, the section's "start"/"center" trigger points didn't line up with when the screenshot itself was actually passing through the viewport — producing a decoupled, uneven feel (looked like a slow shrink then a sudden jump) rather than a smooth grow-as-it-scrolls-into-view. This revision tracks the screenshot's own element instead.

The project uses Lenis (`src/components/SmoothScroll.tsx`, `lerp: 0.1`) for eased smooth-scrolling site-wide: any scroll input (even a light, brief one) is smoothed by Lenis into the actual scroll position over roughly half a second to a second, rather than jumping. A third visual pass found that with the screenshot's growth range spanning only `["start end", "center center"]` on `previewRef` (roughly the element's own height plus half the viewport), even a very light touch of the wheel/trackpad — smoothed out by Lenis — covered a large fraction of that range, making the screenshot appear to grow "on its own" from barely any input. The range is widened in this revision so a light touch covers a smaller fraction of it.

Reference behavior: the background reveals a shifting vertical slice of a tall photo (sky → flower field) as the hero scrolls, confined entirely to the hero section; the product screenshot noticeably scales up (~0.6 → ~1.15) as it scrolls into and further past the viewport's center — no viewport pinning, no full-bleed breakout on the screenshot.

See `proposal.md` - Why/What Changes for motivation and scope. See `specs/landing/hero-scroll-reveal/spec.md` for the exact required behaviors.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Confine the background artwork to the hero section's own bounds (never renders outside it, at any scroll position) while giving it a vertical scroll-linked reveal — driven by `useScroll({ target: sectionRef, ... })` spanning the background's full reveal range.
- Give the product preview screenshot a single, in-place `useScroll`/`useTransform` scale animation, tracked against the screenshot's own element (not the whole section) so growth stays synced to when it's actually visible — from entering the viewport to being roughly centered.
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by disabling both the background's reveal and the screenshot's scale animation.

**Non-Goals:**
- Any viewport pinning/sticky mechanic or full-width/edge-to-edge breakout of the screenshot — explicitly rejected in favor of the simpler in-place scale.
- Redesigning the `mockup-browser` chrome/toolbar visual treatment — it scales as-is with the screenshot.
- Bespoke mobile/small-viewport choreography — the same confined-parallax + in-place-scale mechanic applies at all breakpoints; tuning the exact reveal/scale ranges for small screens is left as a follow-up if it proves janky in testing.
- Changing the rotating-word headline (`TextDoodle` + word cycling) or CTAs — out of scope, untouched by this change.

## Decisions

### 1. Background: confined parallax via `overflow-hidden` clipping + oversized inner layer + `useTransform` translateY
The background stays two nested elements. The outer wrapper is `absolute inset-0 overflow-hidden` inside the hero `<section>` (which is itself `position: relative`) — this is what physically clips the background to the hero's bounds; nothing painted inside it can ever be visible outside the section's box, at any scroll position. Inside that clip, an inner `motion.div` is rendered deliberately taller than the clip window (e.g. `h-[130%]`, offset `top-[-12%]`) and holds the `<Image object-cover object-top>`. That inner layer's `y` is driven by `useTransform` off `useScroll({ target: sectionRef, offset: ["start start", "end start"] })`, animating a vertical translate (`"0%" → "-18%"`, tuned visually) as the hero scrolls — this is what reveals more of the tall source photo (moving from the sky near the top of the range toward the flower field lower in the image). Only `y` is animated; scale and opacity are never touched.
- Alternative considered (tried and rejected after visual testing): plain CSS `position: fixed`. A fixed element positions relative to the viewport, not its containing section, so `overflow-hidden` on the section cannot clip it — it rendered across the whole page. It also had no movement at all, so the crop never changed and the flower field was never revealed. Both problems are inherent to `position: fixed` for this use case, not just something needing extra CSS (containing-block tricks like `transform`/`filter`/`contain` on the ancestor. would be needed to clip a fixed descendant, and even then it still wouldn't reveal more of the image without added JS movement) — the confined+translate approach solves both directly.
- Alternative considered: driving `background-position`/`background-attachment: fixed` on a real CSS background instead of a Next.js `<Image>`. Rejected — the project already renders this artwork via `next/image` for its optimization pipeline (`priority`, `sizes`, responsive `srcset`); switching to a plain CSS background would lose that without adding capability the transform-based approach doesn't already provide.

### 2. Screenshot growth via its own `useScroll` + `useTransform` scale, tracked on its own element, over a widened range
A dedicated `previewRef` is placed on the screenshot's own wrapper `motion.div`, and `useScroll({ target: previewRef, offset: ["start end", "end center"] })` tracks progress against that element specifically — progress 0 when the screenshot's top edge reaches the bottom of the viewport (it starts entering from below) and progress 1 when the screenshot's *bottom* edge reaches the viewport's center (roughly its own height further than the "center center" checkpoint used previously). `useTransform(scrollYProgress, [0, 1], [0.6, 1.15])` drives `style={{ scale }}` directly on that wrapper — no new full-bleed container, no `transformOrigin` change needed beyond the default (center), no viewport pinning.
- Alternative considered (tried and rejected after visual testing): track the same range against `sectionRef` (the whole hero, `["start end", "center start"]`). Rejected — the hero section's height is dominated by the headline/sub-headline/CTAs above the screenshot, so the section's own "start"/"center" trigger points don't correspond to when the screenshot itself is passing through the viewport. This produced a visually decoupled, uneven animation (looked like a slow shrink then a sudden jump) rather than a smooth grow-as-it-scrolls-into-view. Tracking the screenshot's own element removes that mismatch entirely.
- Alternative considered (tried and rejected after visual testing): `["start end", "center center"]` on `previewRef`. Correctly synced to the element's own visibility, but the resulting scroll distance was short enough that Lenis's eased smoothing (see Context) turned a light, brief scroll input into a large, "on its own"-looking swing through the range. `["start end", "end center"]` covers roughly the element's own height more of scroll distance, so the same light input now covers a proportionally smaller fraction of it.
- Alternative considered: keep the earlier `["start start", "end start"]` range spanning the whole section. Rejected for the same reason as the `sectionRef` alternative above.
- Alternative considered: pin the section while scaling (previous design). Rejected per updated requirements — no pinning, no breakout; the effect must stay in normal document flow.
- Alternative considered: reduce Lenis's global `lerp` value instead of widening this range. Rejected for this change — that would alter the smooth-scroll feel of the entire site, not just this one effect; widening the range is a scoped fix.
- Initial scale lowered from `0.85` to `0.6` per visual feedback — `0.85` read as barely different from full size; `0.6` gives a clearly perceptible "starts small, grows in" effect.

### 3. Reduced motion: keep both effects at a stable state, no animation
`useReducedMotion()` (framer-motion) gates both `useTransform` outputs. When `true`, the background's `y` is pinned to `"0%"` (its resting crop, no scroll-linked shift) and the screenshot's `scale` is pinned to `1`, instead of either tracking its respective `scrollYProgress`. Both effects share the same reduced-motion flag from a single `useReducedMotion()` call.

### 4. Remove the prior prototype's background scale/opacity transforms and re-scope both transforms
The `bgScale` and `bgOpacity` motion values from the earlier prototype are removed entirely — the background now only ever animates `y`. The `previewScale` transform is kept but re-scoped across several revisions: its `useScroll` target moved from `sectionRef` to a dedicated `previewRef` (Decision 2), its offset range widened from `["start end", "center center"]` to `["start end", "end center"]`, and its output range changed from `[0.92, 1.25]` to `[0.85, 1.15]` to `[0.6, 1.15]`. Its wrapping `motion.div` stays exactly where the current `RevealItem` places it (no full-bleed breakout).

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **[Risk] Reveal range/percentages feel off on first pass** → `h-[130%]`/`top-[-12%]` (oversizing) and `"0%" → "-18%"` (translate) are starting points; too little oversizing/translate and the flower field never fully appears, too much and the horizontal crop gets tighter than intended. Mitigation: tune visually via `/run` rather than guessing further in planning — this is the main remaining tuning task.
- **[Risk] Scale range/trigger points feel off on first pass** → `["start end", "end center"]` and `[0.6, 1.15]` are starting points for the screenshot; the widened range reduces but doesn't eliminate sensitivity to a light Lenis-smoothed scroll. Mitigation: tune visually via `/run` (further widen if still too sensitive, narrow if it now feels sluggish) rather than guessing further in planning.
- Background bleed-through into later sections (a risk in the earlier `position: fixed` revision) is resolved by construction here: the background can never paint outside the hero section's `overflow-hidden` box, regardless of what backgrounds later sections do or don't have. No further mitigation needed.
- Screenshot growth decoupled from its own visibility (a risk in the earlier `sectionRef`-tracked revision) is resolved by construction here: tracking `previewRef` directly means progress 0→1 always corresponds to the screenshot's own entry/centering, regardless of how much content sits above it in the section. No further mitigation needed.
- **[Trade-off] Full "1.15" scale is reached slightly past dead-center (bottom-at-viewport-center rather than element-center-at-viewport-center)** → accepted trade-off for the wider, less Lenis-sensitive range; the element is still substantially visible (its upper portion) at that point for typical hero screenshot heights. Verify visually — if too much of the screenshot is clipped above the viewport at full scale, narrow the range slightly rather than reverting to the more sensitive one.
- **[Trade-off] No dramatic full-bleed takeover moment** → accepted per the corrected reference; the screenshot effect is intentionally subtle (a gentle in-place scale), not a cinematic full-viewport reveal.
